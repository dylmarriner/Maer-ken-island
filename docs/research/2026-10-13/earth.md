## 0. Bottom line

1. The living world today is: one climate-driven plant carbon field, one assumed grazer carbon pool per land cell, and people who "eat" by deleting plant carbon from the cell. Everything else a person on Earth lives from (game, fish, shellfish, crops, livestock, fruit and nuts, honey, wild plant staples, a soil that feeds the plants) does not exist, and several things that do exist are computed and then thrown away.
2. Three defects are worse than the listed deviations because they are structural, not parameter errors:
   a. NPP is computed once, at bootstrap (ecology.rs:478), and never again. It does not follow weather, season, soil moisture, fertiliser, grazing or fire. Biomass relaxes to NPP x residence time with annual constants (ecology.rs:524-557).
   b. People's food is "PlantFood" carved out of whole-cell biomass (life.rs:1268-1296, materials.rs:383-413). A forest cell's wood, bark and leaves count as 11% carbon food (materials.rs:131-136). There is no edible fraction, no fruit, seed, tuber, grass-leaf distinction, no season. A founder can live on a forest of trees; no real person can.
   c. The grazer layer's flows are discarded: life.rs:1382 calls step_with_grazers and ignores the returned GrazerFlows. Faeces and dead grazers go nowhere (no detritus, no soil). Detritus is a ledger sink (Reservoir::DetritusCarbon, materials.rs:586, local_vegetation.rs:277) that nothing ever decomposes regionally.
3. Realism checks I computed (DERIVED, inputs sourced): the model's grazer carbon (about 1.5 g C/m2 mean, D41) is about 3x the whole introduced-mammal biomass of an NZ podocarp-broadleaf forest (0.44 g C/m2), about 5x below sheep-and-beef pasture livestock (7.9 g C/m2) and about 13x below dairy pasture (20 g C/m2). It eats 5% of NPP; managed NZ dairy pasture eats about 70-90% of pasture growth. So one number is both too high for wild forest and far too low for farmland, which is the point: grazing must be a per-biome parameter and farmland must be a different regime (section 2.2).
4. Ranking by what matters to the people on the island is in section 3; the ordered plan with files, pack items and tests is in section 4. Short form: (1) farm-and-forage food base (edible fractions, seasonal pasture, crops, livestock, with cited yields) and the soil nutrients that set them; (2) game and hunting; (3) fish and shellfish; (4) predators/cascades/birds; (5) fire, succession, dispersal, pollinators, disease.

### 1.4 Water cycle and climate into life

Hydrology (soil bucket, rivers, lakes, ET) and weather/climate (diurnal range, orographic rain, SST, tides) are the island's most developed layers (hydrology.rs, climate.rs, weather.rs, ocean.rs, synoptic.rs; D3-D5, D25-D28, D37-D38). ### 1.5 Food webs and disease

Food web: producers -> one grazer pool, nothing above or below it. Detritivores, decomposers, scavengers, predators, omnivores: absent. Disease (plant, animal, human infection): absent; the only mortality is the Siler age hazard (D21), accidents/needs, and the grazers' flat 0.4/yr.

### 2.2 Food web: herbivores, predators, and what fraction of production they use (D41)

Test of the model's five constants against the literature (all from notes_A):
- Fraction of NPP eaten. "aquatic herbivores remove on average 51% of annual primary production, three times more than terrestrial herbivores" (Cyr & Pace 1993, Nature 361:148; https://doi.org/10.1038/361148a0 VERIFIED abstract); terrestrial median 18% (Barbier & Loreau 2019, Ecol Lett, https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13196 VERIFIED, SECONDARY for the number). Cebrian 1999 data as quoted by Konno (arXiv 1412.5773): "0-14% (quartile 25-75% to be 0-6%, average 1.5%) in forests and shrublands together, and 0-65% (quartile 25-75% to be 1-43%, average 23%) in grasslands" (original https://doi.org/10.1086/303244; per-biome numbers VERIFIED SECONDARY). Cebrian: "Communities composed of more nutritional plants ... lose higher percentages of production to herbivores". Verdict: 5% is above forest means and far below grassland means; make it biome-specific (forest about 1.5%, grass about 23%, farmland by stocking rate).
- Managed grazing is a different regime. DERIVED from verified NZ figures: dairy 2.76-2.82 cows/ha (DairyNZ Dairy Statistics 2023-24, https://connect.dairynz.co.nz/2023-24_Dairy_Statistics/national-dairy-statistics.html VERIFIED), a base cow eats "4500 kg DM ... per year" (Otago Regional Council memo, https://orc.govt.nz/media/nkyfhcv5/memo-stocking-rate-definitions-and-threshold-values-nov-23.pdf VERIFIED, secondary: council memo citing Parker 1998 and Wheeler 2018) = about 12.6 t DM/ha/yr eaten from 14-18 t DM/ha/yr growth (Waikato), i.e. 70-90% utilisation. One stock unit is 550 kg DM; sheep-and-beef hill country carries 7-13 SU/ha, finishing 8-15 (Beef+Lamb NZ Sheep & Beef Farm Survey 2021-22, https://beeflambnz.com/sites/default/files/2023-09/2022%20WNI.pdf VERIFIED).
- Net production efficiency. van der Meer 2021 (Ecol Lett, https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13633 VERIFIED, SECONDARY for Golley 1968): "about 20% of the assimilated energy by poikilothermic populations is manifested as production, whereas this figure is as low as 2% in homeothermic bird and mammal populations." The model's 5% is between; use about 2% for mammal and bird grazers, up to about 20% for invertebrates (so a separate insect/invertebrate grazer group).
- Mortality. Flat 0.4/yr is not mass-dependent. Hatton et al. 2019 PNAS: "growth and mortality rates scale similarly both within and across groups, with exponents of +/-1/4." https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1900492116 VERIFIED. Duncan, Forsyth & Hone 2007 (Ecology 88:324): r_max scales as M^-0.25 across 1197 mammal species (VERIFIED abstract). Fenchel 1974 "n has a value of about -0.275". https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00384576 VERIFIED abstract.
- Equilibrium pool 0.00625 x NPP is internally consistent (0.05 x 0.05 / 0.4) and within 1.5x of 18% x 2% / 0.4 = 0.009 x NPP (DERIVED, notes_A item 11), but see the observed densities below.

### 2.7 Succession, fire, disturbance, seed dispersal and regeneration

- Fire regime (Perry, Wilmshurst & McGlone 2014, NZ J Ecology 38:157, https://newzealandecology.org/nzje/3198/pdf VERIFIED): "fire frequencies were low across most of New Zealand, with the notable exception of some wetland systems"; "in the order of centuries, but possibly millennia apart at the same site"; Te Ara "Typically they happen only every 1,000-2,000 years"; "Lightning (natural ignition) is responsible for just 0.1% of fire events and burned area"; "the number of rural wildfires ... increased from 1200 to a little over 4000 events per year, but the amount of burned area remained relatively constant"; "around 2400 ha per year are burned with an average fire size around 14 ha" (conservation estate); controlled burn-offs "around 60 000 ha per year"; "Because few of New Zealand's indigenous plant species show any real adaptation to fire, the greatly increased fire activity ... resulted in widespread, and in some cases permanent, shifts"; "some 3.3 million hectares of forest were burned in the period 1830-1873"; Maori burned about 6.7 Mha. Use: fire is human-ignited, area heavy-tailed (mean 14 ha), forest is fire-intolerant (fire-trap feedback to bracken/grass). Fire danger indices (FWI, Scion rating) and fuel-moisture thresholds: NOT FOUND.
- Succession clock (Te Ara Forest succession and regeneration, https://teara.govt.nz/en/forest-succession-and-regeneration/print VERIFIED): "Within 10-20 years a shrubland develops", "Manuka is senile at 30-50 years", "kanuka eventually replaces manuka because it grows taller and lives longer (up to 150 years)", broadleaves "dominate the forest for 300-400 years", conifers 600-2,000 yr. Kanuka height "34.4 (1 - 0.993^Age) m", soil depth "from an average of 20 cm at age 10 yr to 58 cm at age 70 yr", and "continued removal of mostly palatable potential successors by grazing will stall their progression to tall forest" (Smale, McLeod & Smale 1997, https://newzealandecology.org/nzje/2010_.pdf VERIFIED).
- Landslides on steep land: soil recovery "from 3.5 mm yr-1 over the first 40 years after slipping to 1.2 mm yr-1 over the following 50 years"; slope threshold about 28 degrees (De Rose 2009, Eurasian J For Res 12:9, https://eprints.lib.hokudai.ac.jp/dspace/handle/2115/38223 VERIFIED). - Pre-human cover target: "the reduction of forest cover from 85-90% to 25% of the land area" (Perry et al. 2014); native forest "6.2 million hectares (23% of the country)" (Te Ara); LCDB 2018 (Stats NZ via Figure.NZ, DERIVED sums): exotic grassland 10.6 Mha, native forest 6.3 Mha, scrub 2.0 Mha, exotic forest 1.8 Mha, cropping/horticulture 0.47 Mha. (Sources disagree on native-forest area: 6.2-6.3 vs 7.8 Mha in the carbon summary; pick a definition.)
- Seed dispersal and masting: Kelly & Sork 2002 (https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ecolsys.33.020602.095433 VERIFIED abstract) "The global pattern of masting shows highest seed crop variability at mid latitudes and in the Southern Hemisphere"; Kelly 2013 dT cue above; beech masting 3-5 years (Dilks, DOC). Dispersal kernel distributions (m) and seed rain per m2: NOT FOUND (Nathan & Muller-Landau 2000 not opened).

### 2.8 Disease and health of living things

## 3. Ranking of gaps by importance to the people on the island

Criterion: how much of a person's survival, comfort and economy depends on it, and how wrong the current model is. Scores are my judgement from the code inventory and the sources (not sourced numbers).

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Edible food base: no edible fraction (food = any biomass), no crops, no pasture-to-livestock, no seasons, NPP static | It is the only food the founders have, and the owner's estate farms (beef, dairy, sheep, pigs, market gardens) depend on seasonal pasture, feed budgets and crop yields. A real person cannot live on forest wood-carbon | Wrong in kind: a forest cell is edible at 11% of carbon; no winter feed gap; no harvest per hectare numbers |

## 6. Corrections to assumptions already in the project or in my brief (found during research)

1. NZ fisheries have no default 40% B0 target (the Ministry "deliberately omitted 40% B0 as a default target"; limits are 20% B0 soft, 10% B0 hard).
2. Klein et al. 2007: the abstract gives 87 of the leading crops and 35% of production, not "75%".
3. RothC DPM decays at k = 10/yr (mean life 0.1 yr), not 0.165 yr.
4. NZ Ecklonia kelp NPP is 374-615 g C/m2/yr; the generic 800-2000 is unverified.
5. The widely quoted red-deer r = 2.33 is an immigration artefact (the source says so); use 0.33-0.56 as weak upper values.
6. The Brose metabolic coefficient 0.314 applies to invertebrates only (ectotherm vertebrates 0.88, endotherms 3.22).
7. Sutherland et al. 2000 is the Conservation Ecology 4(1):16 paper (not "Parameterising dispersal").
8. The model's grazer pool is simultaneously too high for wild forest and too low for pasture; and NZ pasture takes 70-90% of growth, not 5%.
9. Pauly & Christensen's PPR is 6.2% (FishBase copy) or 8.0% (repository abstract); unresolved.
10. NZ native forest area is 6.2-6.3 Mha or 7.8 Mha depending on definition; MfE tall-forest carbon (257.7 t C/ha) includes soil.
