## 1. Trophic transfer, efficiencies, fraction of NPP eaten

1. 3. Production efficiency, endotherm vs ectotherm populations | ~20% of assimilated energy becomes production in poikilotherm populations vs "as low as 2%" in homeothermic bird and mammal populations (Golley 1968) | "Golley (1968), for example observed that about 20% of the assimilated energy by poikilothermic populations is manifested as production, whereas this figure is as low as 2% in homeothermic bird and mammal populations." | van der Meer J (2021) Ecol Lett, citing Golley 1968, Humphreys 1979 | https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13633 (full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7821020/) | VERIFIED (SECONDARY for Golley/Humphreys numbers) | Net production efficiency NPE (production / assimilation). 4. ## 3. Allometry: Damuth, Kleiber, MTE, rates

18. Damuth's law (equal energy use) | density ~ 1/(individual metabolic need); local population energy use independent of body size (mammalian herbivores) | "Density is related approximately reciprocally to individual metabolic requirements, indicating that the energy used by the local population of a species in the community is independent of its body size." | Damuth J (1981) Nature 290:699-700 | https://doi.org/10.1038/290699a0 (abstract read at https://www.nature.com/articles/290699a0) | VERIFIED (abstract; exponent -0.75 is not stated in the abstract) | Density D = D0 x M^-0.75 for herbivore species; equivalently each species pool gets an equal energy share, so with N species in a guild, split NPP-derived energy equally per species-size class.

19. ## Ten-line summary of the most useful verified numbers
1. Trophic transfer efficiency 10% mean, re-estimated (Pauly & Christensen 1995); marine ecosystem TE varies and declines with warming (Eddy 2021).
2. Terrestrial herbivores consume ~18% of NPP (median, Cyr & Pace via Barbier & Loreau 2019); aquatic 51%. Forest+shrub mean 1.5% (0-14%); grassland mean 23% (0-65%) (Cebrian 1999 via Konno). The model's 5% cap is low for grassland/pasture, high for forest means.
3. Net production efficiency: ~2% (birds/mammals) vs ~20% (poikilotherms) (Golley 1968 via van der Meer 2021). The model's 5% is plausible; grazer pool = 0.05 x 0.05 / 0.4 = 0.00625 x NPP is internally consistent, 18%/2%/0.4 would give 0.009 x NPP.
4. Mortality and growth scale as M^(+-1/4) across eukaryotes (Hatton 2019); r_max ~ M^-0.25 (Duncan 2007), Fenchel r_m = aW^-0.275 with W in g; so a flat 0.4/yr should be mass-dependent.
5. Damuth: density ~ reciprocal of metabolic need (energy use independent of mass, Damuth 1981); FMR slopes mammals 0.734 (Nagy 1999); life-history slopes (placentals): gestation 0.24, first reproduction 0.27, lifespan 0.21, annual fecundity -0.26 (Hamilton 2011).
6. Carnivore biomass = 90 kg per 10,000 kg of prey (0.9%), number ~ 1/mass (Carbone & Gittleman 2002); predator-prey biomass exponent ~3/4 (Hatton 2015); bottom-heavy pyramid on land, inverted in oceans (~1 Gt C producers : ~5 Gt C consumers) (Bar-On 2018).
7. Global biomass: plants 450 Gt C, terrestrial arthropods 0.2, wild mammals 0.007, wild birds 0.002 (Bar-On 2018); wild land mammals ~20 Mt wet, ~3 kg per person (Greenspoon 2023).
8. Bioenergetic ATN parameters: assimilation e = 0.45 (herbivory) / 0.85 (carnivory); metabolic coefficient a_x = 0.314 (invertebrates), 0.88 (ectotherm vertebrates), 3.22 (endotherms, Brose); revised lower (0.13, 0.274, 2.27) by Blyth 2026; max consumption y = 8 (invert) / 4 (fish); Hill exponent 1.2-1.5.
9. Rall 2012 functional response (n = 648; mass in mg): attack ln a0 = -16.82, E = 0.44 eV, consumer exponent 0.47, resource exponent 0.15; handling ln h0 = 8.30, E = -0.27 eV, consumer exponent -0.48, resource exponent 0.34. MTE null: E = 0.65 eV.
10. Dispersal (Sutherland 2000): mammal median distance (km) = 2.04 M^0.67 (M in kg), maximum = 6.46 M^0.68, max is ~5.8x median, kernel P(>D) = exp(-D/1.5) in median units; PanTHERIA species means and King 2023 give NZ-relevant densities (rodents 100-170/ha in grassland, mouse 27-50/ha after mast, ferret up to 10.1/km2) and breeding data; Ecosim-type foraging-arena / ratio-dependent responses explain the stabilised donor-controlled behaviour of ecosystem models (Heath 2014).
