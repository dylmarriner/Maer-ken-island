## How the three sessions worked

- **Split by area**, agreed by message at the start and re-checked before each new item:
  - session `38` held the main checkout and the desktop app (`island_ui`, Phase 5);
  - session `a9` took the engine PRs first, then Phase 3 Task 2 and the climate items;
  - session `ce` took the review follow-ups for the deep-time spin-up and timber drying, then the
    realism steps R2 and R4 and timber §1c.

  Each session worked in its own git worktree, never in another's.
- **Memory is the bottleneck.** Three sessions building at once on 8 cores and 15 GB ran the
  machine out of memory: Claude Code killed a workspace test run, two local runs of the week's
  release test and a release `island_ui` build. The rule that followed:
  - one workspace-wide or release build at a time, at `-j4` or less, announced before and after;
  - everything else is single-crate and debug;
  - **the slow tier runs on CI**, dispatched per branch with
    `gh workflow run ci.yml --ref <branch>` (the `slow tests (nightly)` job runs on
    `workflow_dispatch`).

  A re-pin of `WEEK_DIGEST` can be read straight from that job's log. The week test prints the
  new digest after every other check has passed.
- **A push cancels a dispatched run on the same branch** (the CI concurrency group is
  workflow + ref). Push review fixes after the slow run finishes, or dispatch again.
- **Deviation ids must be consecutive** (`deviation_register`), so two open PRs can hold the
  same next id. #31 took D56 when it merged, and #34 was rebased to D57. #42 also holds D57;
  whichever of #34 and #42 merges second renumbers.
- **`WEEK_DIGEST` collisions.** a9's #40 merged and moved main's week from `54b48e5b…` to
  `ff6eeb03…`. #34 (the gas stove), #42 (R3) and #45 (mining) each move it again. Every PR
  merged after another one that moved the week re-runs the week and re-pins on top of main.
  Session ce rebased #29, #30, #34 and #37 onto `ff6eeb03…` and re-ran the slow tier on each.
  #29, #30 and #37 leave the digest alone: no wood is felled in the week, and their slow tiers
  passed again on top of `ff6eeb03…` (runs 38490358089, 38490365793 and 38490375182).

- **A split-only rate for splitting firewood with an axe.** Granqvist (1993, Teho 43(4)) gives
  0.95 m³/h, but for chainsaw crosscutting plus axe splitting together. The only split-only
  figure is a one-person blog test. Splitting stays open (WORK_REMAINING §1c and R4).
- **NZ LPG-only cooking energy.** HEEP's 900 kWh is an all-fuel average; BRANZ SR221's cooking
  chapter was not reachable. D57 (#34) says so.
- **The Pandolf (1977) paper itself.** The equation and terrain factors are transcribed from the
  USARIEM review that reproduces them (Richmond, Potter & Santee 2015). The pack's note says so.
